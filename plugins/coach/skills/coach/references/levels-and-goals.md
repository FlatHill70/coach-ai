# Levels and goal playbooks

These are evidence-informed starting points, not dogma. The user's own data overrides the tables. When you deviate, say which data point made you do it.

## Levels

| Level | Who | Progression speed | What matters most |
|---|---|---|---|
| `novice` | Never trained or returning after years | Session to session | Learning movements, building the habit, not getting hurt, not overthinking |
| `beginner` | < ~1 year consistent | Week to week | Linear/double progression on a few basics, consistency, protein |
| `intermediate` | 1–3 years | Every few weeks | Volume management, exercise selection, weak points, planned variation |
| `advanced` | 3+ years | Every few months | Periodisation, specialisation blocks, fatigue management, recovery |
| `elite` | Competitive | Per block or season | Peaking, competition specifics, working with their coach/federation rules |

**Level markers** (for males of average height, very rough; scale down for females and lighter people): an intermediate can do a bench press ≈ bodyweight, squat ≈ 1.25–1.5× and deadlift ≈ 1.5–1.75× for a single, or 10+ strict pull-ups. Use these only as a sanity check on self-report. `workouts balance` › `relativeStrength` computes the bands.

### Novice specifics (never trained)

- Explain what a set, rep, rest and RIR are the first time they appear. Use one new concept per session.
- 2–3 full-body sessions per week, 30–50 min, 5–7 exercises, 2–3 sets each. Use machines, dumbbells and supported variations. Teach the barbell lifts only when they want to and technique allows.
- Intensity: finish every set feeling you had 3 more good reps (RIR 3), then move to RIR 2 after a few weeks.
- Progress: add reps first, then load. Expect fast early gains, mostly from technique and coordination.
- Gym anxiety is real. Give a simple plan for the first visit (where things are, what to do if a machine is busy, that nobody is watching). Offer a home or bodyweight version if the gym is a barrier.
- Success in month 1 is attendance, not numbers.

### Elite specifics

They probably have a coach and federation rules. Your role is analyst and sparring partner: volume and intensity distribution, readiness trends, peaking blocks (taper volume 40–60% over 1–2 weeks while keeping intensity), attempt selection from recent e1RMs, and weight-class management (no dangerous water cuts; they follow their federation and medical guidance).

## Goal playbooks

### Muscle gain (`muscle_gain`)
- Volume 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week (novices/beginners do well with 6–12). Each muscle trained ≥2×/week. Reps 5–30 taken close to failure (RIR 0–3). Practical split: compounds 5–10 reps, accessories 8–15, small muscles 10–20.
- Surplus to gain 0.25–0.5 %/week (beginners at the top, advanced at the bottom). Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg.
- Progress = load or reps up on the same exercises, plus waist/arm/thigh measurements.

### Fat loss (`fat_loss`)
- Keep training heavy enough to keep muscle: keep the loads, reduce volume a little (~⅓) if recovery suffers. Daily steps are the big lever for energy expenditure.
- Deficit to lose 0.5–1 %/week (leaner people slower). Protein 1.8–2.4 g/kg. Diet breaks (1–2 weeks at maintenance) every 8–12 weeks help adherence.
- Progress = weight trend + waist + strength held. If strength drops fast, the deficit is too aggressive.
- **Not for under-18s** (teen mode) or people with ED history (`safety.md`).

### Recomposition (`recomp`)
- Best for novices, people returning to training, and higher body fat. Train like muscle gain at maintenance calories (± 10%). Protein 1.8–2.4 g/kg.
- The scale barely moves, so judge by waist down, strength up and photos. Re-evaluate at 8–12 weeks. If both stall, pick a direction (gain or cut).

### Strength (`strength`)
- Specificity: practise the lifts that matter (e.g. squat/bench/deadlift or the user's choice) 2–4×/week. Main work at 1–6 reps, 75–90% 1RM, RPE 6–9. Accessory hypertrophy work at 6–12 reps.
- Model by level: beginner linear progression → intermediate weekly undulation (heavy/medium/light) → advanced block periodisation (accumulation → intensification → realisation/peak).
- Maintenance or a small surplus. Test e1RMs from top sets, not maxes, except at planned test days.

### General health (`general_health`)
- Minimum effective dose: 2 full-body strength sessions/week covering squat, hinge, push, pull and carry, plus 150–300 min/week of moderate cardio (or 75–150 vigorous), plus daily steps. Mobility as needed.
- No tracking required. Habits: protein at each meal, vegetables, sleep 7–9 h.

### Endurance / hybrid (`endurance`)
- Strength 2×/week (heavy-ish, low volume: 3–5 sets of 3–6 on basics plus unilateral leg work), placed away from key endurance sessions (or on the same day, strength after endurance, with 6+ h between).
- Carbohydrate 5–7 g/kg on heavy days. Watch for interference: if leg strength drops, cut gym leg volume, not intensity.

### Sport performance (`performance`)
- Ask about the season (off-season / pre-season / in-season). Off-season: build strength and muscle. Pre-season: power and speed (jumps, throws, sprints, heavy-light contrasts). In-season: maintain strength with 1–2 short sessions, low soreness.
- Coordinate with their sport coach's load. Injury-prevention priorities by sport (e.g. hamstrings/Nordics for sprinters and footballers, shoulders for overhead athletes).

## Realistic rates (natural, for setting goals)

- **Muscle gain** (approximate, under ideal conditions): year 1 ≈ 0.5–1 % of body mass per month, year 2 ≈ half that, year 3+ ≈ half again. Women ≈ half the absolute values of men.
- **Strength**: novices can add load every session for weeks. Beginners add +2.5–5 kg/month on upper-body compounds and more on lower body. Intermediates manage +5–10 % per year on a lift. Advanced lifters manage 1–3 % per year.
- **Fat loss**: 0.5–1 %/week sustainable. Faster than that eats muscle and adherence.
- **First pull-up** from zero: typically 6–16 weeks with negatives, assisted pull-ups and rows.

If a goal implies a faster rate, say so kindly. Offer a realistic target plus a stretch target, and keep the user's motivation intact.
