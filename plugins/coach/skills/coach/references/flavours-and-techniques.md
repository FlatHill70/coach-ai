# Flavours and techniques to discover

Read with `recipes.md`. Coach recipes should also teach: each time, the user can discover a spice, a herb or a way of cooking they haven't tried. Explain it in one line, in plain words ("ras el hanout: a Moroccan blend, warm and slightly sweet, not hot").

## Spices and blends

Amounts are for 2 servings. Ground spices taste better toasted 30–60 s in the oil before the rest goes in; blends with sugar or garlic powder burn above ~200 °C, so add them at the end or lower the heat.

- **Smoked paprika (pimentón de la Vera)**, sweet or hot: Spanish classic. Potatoes, eggs, octopus, chicken, chickpeas. 1 tsp. Burns fast: off the heat or at the end.
- **Cumin (comino)**: earthy. Chickpeas, lentils, lamb, carrots, Mexican and Indian food. 1/2 tsp.
- **Ras el hanout**: Moroccan blend (cumin, coriander, cinnamon, ginger, turmeric, cardamom, nutmeg, clove; each brand differs). Warm, not hot. Chicken, lamb, roast vegetables, chickpeas, couscous, salmon. 1–2 tsp. Mixed into Greek yoghurt it makes a dip.
- **Curry powder**: mild Indian-style blend (turmeric, coriander, cumin, fenugreek, chilli). Chicken, prawns, white fish, lentils, cauliflower, rice. 1–2 tsp, always fried in the oil first. With coconut milk or yoghurt it makes a sauce.
- **Garam masala**: warm Indian blend, added at the end. Chicken tikka, lentils, chickpeas.
- **Turmeric (cúrcuma)**: colour and earthiness; rice, eggs, cauliflower. A pinch of black pepper with it.
- **Curry paste (red, green, yellow)**: Thai, hotter and fresher. Fish, prawns, chicken with coconut milk.
- **Za'atar**: thyme, sumac and sesame. Eggs, flatbread, chicken, yoghurt, roast vegetables.
- **Sumac**: lemony and tangy, no heat. Onion, salads, chicken, fish.
- **Five-spice (cinco especias)**: Chinese, anise and cinnamon. Pork, duck, chicken thighs.
- **Cajun / Creole**: paprika, garlic, onion, cayenne, thyme. Chicken, prawns, potatoes, corn.
- **Chipotle**: smoked jalapeño, smoky and hot. Tacos, beans, chicken, mayo or yoghurt sauces.
- **Cinnamon (canela)**: sweet and savoury: tagines, meatballs, oats, yoghurt.
- **Ginger (jengibre)**, fresh or ground: stir-fries, fish, chicken, carrots.
- **Herbes de Provence / Italian herbs**: dried mix for tomato, chicken, vegetables, potatoes.
- **Shichimi togarashi / furikake**: Japanese, on rice, eggs, salmon.
- **Saffron (azafrán)**: paella, rice, fish stews. A few threads soaked in warm water.

## Herbs

Woody herbs (rosemary, thyme, oregano, bay) go in at the start and survive long cooking and the oven. Soft herbs (parsley, coriander, basil, dill, mint, chives) go in at the end or raw, or they lose everything.

- **Parsley (perejil)**: the Spanish all-rounder. Fish, salsa verde, garlic sauces, potatoes, eggs. Fresh at the end; with garlic and oil it's a "picada" for anything.
- **Coriander (cilantro)**: Mexican, Indian, Thai, Moroccan. Some people taste soap: ask before using it.
- **Basil (albahaca)**: tomato, mozzarella, pasta, pesto.
- **Dill (eneldo)**: salmon, yoghurt, cucumber, potatoes.
- **Mint (hierbabuena/menta)**: lamb, yoghurt, peas, couscous, salads.
- **Rosemary (romero)** and **thyme (tomillo)**: roasts, potatoes, chicken, lamb, oven vegetables.
- **Oregano**: tomato, pizza, Greek and Mexican food, adobos.
- **Bay leaf (laurel)**: stews, lentils, rice, fish stock. Remove before serving.
- **Chives (cebollino)**: eggs, potatoes, yoghurt and fresh-cheese sauces.

## Flavour boosters that cost almost no calories

Lemon or lime zest and juice · vinegars (sherry, apple, balsamic) · mustard · garlic and onion · soy sauce (salty: less added salt) · miso · hot sauce · capers, olives, pickles · grated ginger · toasted spices · Greek yoghurt sauces (with herbs, garlic, spice blends) · fresh tomato · roasted peppers. Recommend these first when the user wants more flavour on a light dish.

## Pairings by cuisine

- **Spanish**: garlic, parsley, smoked paprika, saffron, bay, olive oil, sherry vinegar.
- **Moroccan / North African**: ras el hanout, cumin, cinnamon, preserved lemon, coriander, parsley, mint, harissa.
- **Middle Eastern**: za'atar, sumac, cumin, tahini, lemon, mint, yoghurt.
- **Indian**: curry, garam masala, turmeric, cumin, ginger, garlic, coriander, yoghurt.
- **Thai**: curry paste, coconut milk, lime, fish sauce, ginger, chilli, basil.
- **Mexican**: cumin, chipotle, oregano, lime, coriander, chilli.
- **Japanese / Korean**: soy, miso, ginger, sesame, rice vinegar, gochujang, spring onion.
- **Italian**: basil, oregano, garlic, tomato, lemon, parmesan.

## Techniques worth discovering

Each one in one line, so the coach can teach it inside a recipe:

- **Marinade (adobo)**: acid + oil + spices, 30 min to overnight in the fridge; fish 15–30 min at most (the acid "cooks" it).
- **Dry rub**: spices + salt rubbed on before cooking, no liquid. Fast flavour for air fryer and oven.
- **En papillote**: parcel of baking paper with fish or chicken, vegetables, herbs and a splash of liquid; it steams in its own juices, no oil needed.
- **Traybake**: everything on one tray in the oven, cut so it cooks at the same time.
- **Stir-fry (salteado / wok)**: very hot pan, small pieces, everything prepped before you start, 3–6 min total.
- **Poaching (escalfado)**: just below the boil in stock, milk or coconut milk. Tender fish, chicken and eggs.
- **Steaming (al vapor)**: in a pot with a lid or in the microwave with a covered dish and 2 tbsp of water. Fish 5–8 min, vegetables 3–6 min.
- **Braising / stew (guiso)**: brown first, then cook slowly covered in liquid. Cheap cuts turn tender.
- **Glaze**: a sticky sauce (honey-soy, miso, teriyaki) brushed on in the last minutes.
- **Escabeche**: cook, then keep in oil, vinegar, garlic, bay and pepper. Lasts days in the fridge (meal prep).
- **A la sal**: whole fish or meat buried in coarse salt in the oven; juicy, not salty.
- **Picada / sofrito**: Spanish bases. Sofrito = onion, garlic and tomato cooked slowly. Picada = garlic, parsley and nuts pounded, added at the end.
- **Yoghurt sauce**: Greek yoghurt + lemon + garlic + a herb or spice blend. Protein instead of mayo.
- **Crispy coating**: see `recipes.md` › Breaded and battered.
