# Fish and seafood

Read with `recipes.md` whenever the recipe has fish or seafood. Fish cooks fast and dries out fast: give a short time, a doneness check and "check 2 minutes early".

## Doneness and handling

- Done at **63 °C** in the centre, or when the flesh turns opaque and **flakes** with a fork. Salmon and tuna can be served pink in the middle if the fish was frozen first (see Anisakis).
- Prawns: done when they curl into a "C" and turn pink; an "O" means overcooked. Mussels and clams: done when they open; throw away any that stay closed.
- Squid and octopus: either **very fast** (1–2 min on high heat) or **long and slow** (40+ min, or pressure cooker). Anything in between turns rubbery.
- Pat fillets dry before cooking, and start skin side down. Thaw frozen fish in the fridge overnight, never on the counter. Fresh fish keeps 1–2 days in the fridge.

## Anisakis (raw, marinated or barely cooked fish)

Spain's food agency (AESAN): fish eaten **raw, marinated, in vinegar, smoked cold or barely cooked** (boquerones en vinagre, ceviche, sushi, tartar, carpaccio, escabeche suave) must be frozen first. At home: **−20 °C for 5 days**, or −15 °C or lower for at least 4 days, counting 5 days from when it goes in the freezer. If the freezer doesn't reach that, buy it already frozen. Cooking it to 60 °C in the centre for 1 minute is enough on its own.
- Never offer raw-fish recipes during pregnancy or to a weak immune system.

## Mercury

AESAN recommendations: **swordfish (pez espada/emperador), shark (cazón, marrajo, tintorera), bluefin tuna and pike** are off the menu during pregnancy, breastfeeding, when planning a pregnancy, and for children under 10. Ages 10–14: at most 120 g a month of these four. Everyone else: vary species and favour the small ones (sardines, anchovies, mackerel, hake, cod, sea bream). Canned light tuna (skipjack/yellowfin) is fine in normal amounts. Aim for 3–4 fish servings a week, mixing white and oily fish.

## Times by appliance

Air fryer (preheat, single layer, 1 tsp oil brushed on):
- White fish fillet 2 cm (hake, cod): 190–200 °C, 8–10 min
- Salmon fillet ~150 g: 200 °C, 7–9 min, skin down, no flip
- Sardines, whole: 200 °C, 8–10 min, flip at half
- Prawns: 200 °C, 5–7 min, shake at half
- Squid rings: 200 °C, 6–8 min (breaded: 8–10 min)
- Breaded fish fingers or fillets: 200 °C, 8–12 min
- Breaded fish needs oil sprayed on top; delicate fillets go on baking paper with holes.

Griddle / pan (very hot, oiled fish not oiled pan):
- Fillet with skin: 3–4 min skin side, press it flat the first 30 s, 1–2 min other side
- Tuna steak 2 cm: 1–2 min per side for pink, 3 min for cooked through
- Prawns: 1–2 min per side · squid in strips: 1–2 min total on very high heat
- Sardines: 3 min per side

Oven (preheated):
- Fillets: 190–200 °C, 10–15 min depending on thickness
- Whole fish 400–600 g (sea bream, sea bass), on sliced potato and onion: 200 °C, 20–25 min. Done when the dorsal fin pulls out easily.
- En papillote (paper parcel): 200 °C, 10–12 min thin fillets, 13–16 min thick ones
- Salt-baked whole fish (a la sal): 2 kg coarse salt + a little water around a whole fish of ~1 kg, 200 °C, ~20 min per kg

## Spanish and world classics to suggest

- **Merluza en salsa verde**: garlic, parsley, a splash of white wine, clams if there are any.
- **Al ajillo**: prawns or squid with garlic, chilli (guindilla) and olive oil.
- **A la bilbaína**: fish on the griddle, then a sauce of oil, sliced garlic, chilli and a splash of vinegar poured over.
- **Bacalao al pil-pil / a la vizcaína**: salt cod in its own emulsion, or in a dried-pepper (choricero) sauce.
- **Adobo de cazón style** (use hake or cod): marinate in vinegar, cumin, oregano, paprika and garlic, then fry or air-fry breaded.
- **Pescado a la sal**, **dorada a la espalda**, **sardinas a la plancha**, **mejillones al vapor** (3–4 min, lid on).
- **Moroccan chermoula**: parsley, coriander, garlic, cumin, paprika, lemon; brush on fish before the oven.
- **Curry de pescado**: white fish poached 5–6 min in coconut milk with curry paste or powder.
- **Teriyaki / miso salmon**: glaze in the last 2 minutes so the sugar doesn't burn.
- **Fish tacos**: wheat or corn tortillas, griddled or breaded fish, cabbage, lime, yoghurt sauce.
- **Ceviche / boquerones en vinagre / tartar**: only with fish frozen first (see Anisakis).

## Allergies

Fish and crustaceans/molluscs are separate allergens: an allergy to one doesn't rule out the other, but respect what the profile says. Watch hidden fish in surimi, Worcestershire sauce, fish sauce and some stock cubes.
